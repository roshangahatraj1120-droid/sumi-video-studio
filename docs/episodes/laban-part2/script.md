# Laban — Part 2: Who was Laban? (script as received)

A-ROLL. Leave negative space for simple diagrams.

Rudolf von Laban was born in 1879 in Bratislava, then part of the Austro-Hungarian world. Before dance became the
center of his life, he studied art and architecture, including architecture in Paris.

And that background matters. Laban did not only look at movement as performance. He looked at bodies in relation
to space: direction, shape, geometry, weight and time. The same kind of relationships an architect might consider
when thinking about structures.

By the early twentieth century, he had become one of the major figures associated with European modern dance and
the movement culture that broke away from the established vocabulary of classical ballet.

But his project became bigger than choreography. He wanted a system capable of describing movement whether that
movement came from ballet, modern dance - or potentially everyday human action.

In 1928 he published Kinetographie Laban. Today, the American form of this system is commonly known as
Labanotation.

graphics.
