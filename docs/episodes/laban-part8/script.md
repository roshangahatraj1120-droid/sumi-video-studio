# Laban — Part 8 script: Movement Pattern Analysis, corporations and the Pentagon

A-ROLL. Clinical, minimal graphics.

Then Laban's ideas moved even farther away from dance.

One of his proteges, Warren Lamb, developed what became known as Movement Pattern Analysis. The claim
was ambitious: by systematically observing the way someone moved, analysts believed they could identify
characteristic patterns connected to how that person approached decisions.

The Warren Lamb Trust says Movement Pattern Analysis has been used with tens of thousands of people,
particularly in management and organizational settings.

Historian Whitney Laemmli's research traces Laban-derived movement analysis through corporate hiring and
management consulting. Companies associated with this history included firms such as Colgate-Palmolive,
Hewlett-Packard, Kodak and Bank of America.

B-ROLL: Licensed 1950s/60s office imagery; handshake; interview desk; abstract movement-tracking grid.

And then the story gets stranger. According to Laemmli's research, a certified analyst was later hired by
the Pentagon's Office of Net Assessment for a project using movement to study decision-making style.

Among the subjects reportedly analyzed were Saddam Hussein and Osama bin Laden. And Lamb himself later
participated in an analysis involving Vladimir Putin.

information.
