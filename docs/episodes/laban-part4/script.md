# Laban — Part 4 script: politics

Then politics entered the story.

When the Nazis came to power in Germany in 1933, Laban stayed. He was not simply an outsider whose
work happened to exist under the regime. He continued working inside Germany's cultural institutions
and, in 1934, was appointed to lead the Deutsche Tanzbühne - the German Dance Theatre.

Historians still debate how to interpret his relationship with National Socialism, but the larger fact
is clear: for several years, Laban's work operated inside - and benefited from - a cultural system
being reshaped by the Nazi state.

That is an uncomfortable part of his legacy, but it should not be removed from the story.

Laban had already developed what he called movement choirs - large groups of people moving together.
To him, collective movement could still preserve individual expression. To a fascist regime obsessed
with mass spectacle, disciplined bodies and national identity, large-scale movement had very different
political possibilities.
