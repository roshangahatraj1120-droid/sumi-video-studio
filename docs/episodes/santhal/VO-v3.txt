## S1 THE MELA WE REMEMBER
And then came the part we all waited for. The mela! The sweets, the local food, the games, the noise, and the crowd. Walking around with your cousins, spending whatever little money you had. Trying your luck at langur burja. And somehow, even the smallest things could make you so happy.
## S1b
But for me, there was always one thing that made the mela even more special. The Santhal dance. I still remember that excitement. The colorful costumes. The powerful sound of the drums. And the way the dancers moved together, perfectly in rhythm.
## S1c
As a child, I didn't know much about their history. I didn't understand the meaning behind their music or their movements. I just knew one thing. Whenever those drums started playing, I wanted to be there.
## S2 BEHIND THE RHYTHM
Years passed. I grew up. And somewhere along the way, dance became a big part of my life. I started exploring different dance styles, learning their history, and trying to understand the stories behind their movements. But one day, I realized something. I had spent so much time learning about dance from different parts of the world. While knowing so little about the dance that had been right in front of me since childhood. So this time, I came back with a different curiosity. Not just to watch. But to learn. To listen. And to understand.
## S2b
And for the first time, I got to see something I had never really paid attention to as a child. The preparation before the performance. The dancers getting ready. Putting on their traditional costumes. Preparing their instruments. Helping each other with the little details. Every movement, every preparation, building toward that moment when the drums finally begin. As a child, I only saw the performance. But this time, I wanted to understand the people behind it.
## S4 MORE THAN JUST A DANCE
After listening to their stories, watching the performance felt different. The drums. The rhythm. The coordination. The way the dancers move together. There's something powerful about it. You don't need to know every step to feel the energy. And you don't always need to understand the language to connect with the music. That's one of the things I've always loved about dance. It brings people together.
## S4b
But for the Santhal community, this is also a living part of their cultural identity. Something carried through music, movement, celebration, and the people who continue practicing it. And watching the different groups perform, I could see how much energy and dedication went into every performance. Each group bringing its own presence. Each performer contributing to something bigger than themselves. And the crowd? They were enjoying every moment.
## S5 THE THINGS WE LEAVE BEHIND
Standing there, watching the performances, I started thinking about something. How much do we really know about the cultures we grew up around? Many of us have left our villages. Some moved to cities for education. Some left for work. And some are now living thousands of miles away from home. Life moves forward. We get busy. Our priorities change. And slowly, the things that were once part of our everyday lives become memories. The songs we used to hear. The festivals we celebrated. The people we grew up around. And the dances we used to watch.
## S5b
But here's something I've come to understand. Culture doesn't survive through memories alone. It survives when people continue to practice it. When stories are shared. When traditions are taught. And when younger generations have the opportunity to learn from those who came before them. Because when that connection is broken, we risk losing something that cannot simply be recreated. Not just a song. Not just a dance. But a part of someone's history. A part of their identity. A part of who they are.
## S6 THE NEXT GENERATION
But while watching the performances, something else caught my attention. The younger generation. Seeing young dancers performing alongside the older members genuinely made me happy. Because right in front of me, I could see something beautiful happening. A tradition being carried forward. Not just through words. But through movement. Through rhythm. Through participation.
## S6b
And it wasn't only the Santhal performers. People from the crowd were joining in too. Different people. Different backgrounds. All moving together to the same rhythm. And I think that's something worth celebrating. Because cultural preservation doesn't always begin with a big campaign or a grand announcement. Sometimes, it begins with something as simple as showing up. Listening. Learning. Supporting. And allowing the next generation to experience the same traditions that once made our own childhood special.
## S7 FROM WATCHING TO DANCING
And this time, something happened that made the experience even more special for me. I got to join them. The same dance I used to watch with so much excitement as a child. Now, I was dancing alongside them. And honestly? I didn't know all the steps. But that didn't matter. For those few moments, I wasn't thinking about technique. I wasn't thinking about choreography. I wasn't thinking about getting everything right. I was simply enjoying the rhythm. The music. The people. The moment.
## S7b
And looking around, seeing everyone enjoying themselves, I was reminded of something very simple. This is why I fell in love with dance in the first place. Not just to perform. Not just to learn new styles. But to connect. To express. And to share something meaningful with other people.
## S8 THE RHYTHM THAT STAYED
When I was a child, I came to this mela for the excitement. For the sweets. For the games. And for the sound of those drums. Today, I came back with curiosity. And I'm leaving with something more. A deeper appreciation for the people who continue to keep this tradition alive. Because culture isn't something that belongs only to the past. It's alive. In the music. In the movement. In the people who practice it today. And in the younger generations who choose to carry it forward.
## S8b
Maybe we can't bring back our childhood. Maybe we can't go back to those carefree days when the only thing we worried about was how much money we had left to spend at the mela. But we can do something. We can learn about the cultures around us. We can respect the people who preserve them. We can celebrate their traditions without forgetting where they come from. And we can help make sure the next generation gets to experience the same joy that we once did.
## S8c
Because someday, years from now... Another child might be standing in that same mela. Watching those same rhythms come alive. And just like me... They might carry that memory with them wherever life takes them. And maybe, one day... They'll come back too.
