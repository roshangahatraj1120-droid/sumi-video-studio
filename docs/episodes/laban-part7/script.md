# Laban — Part 7 script: New York, the Dance Notation Bureau and the first copyright

A-ROLL. Slightly brighter visual palette.

Across the Atlantic, another branch of Laban's system was developing.

In 1940, Ann Hutchinson Guest and several collaborators helped establish the Dance Notation Bureau in
New York. Its mission was preservation. If choreography could be written precisely enough, a dance did
not have to disappear when the original performers stopped performing it.

Dance scores were created for major choreographers, including works by George Balanchine, Doris
Humphrey, Hanya Holm and others.

B-ROLL: Map of Atlantic, 1940 black and white paper text, archive boxes, licensed vintage Broadway
exteriors, hands turning pages, original notation graphics.

Then, in 1952, something historically important happened. Hanya Holm's choreography for the Broadway
musical Kiss Me, Kate was submitted to the U.S. Copyright Office using a Labanotation score prepared by
Ann Hutchinson Guest.

Holm received what the Library of Congress describes as the first copyright registration claim for a
choreographic work. At the time, choreography was not yet its own explicit category under U.S.
copyright law, so it was registered as a dramatic work.

That changed the philosophical meaning of notation. Movement was no longer only something you could
remember. Now it could help make choreography legally tangible - something that could be archived,
reconstructed and claimed as intellectual property.
