# Laban — Part 10 script: the legacy (closing)

So here is what makes Rudolf Laban's story so fascinating to me.

He began with an artistic problem: movement disappears. A performance exists in time, and then it is gone.

His answer was to make movement legible. To turn the body into something that could be recorded.

But once movement became information, that information did not stay inside dance. It traveled. Into
archives. Factories. Corporate offices. Hospitals. Anthropology. Military analysis. And today,
Laban-inspired movement concepts even appear in areas such as robotics and computer analysis of human
motion.

The technology changed. The question did not: what can we learn by watching the way a body moves?

And maybe that is Laban's most complicated legacy. A system designed partly to preserve something
beautiful also demonstrated that movement could be measured, classified and used. For creativity. For
memory. For healing. For efficiency. And sometimes - for power.
